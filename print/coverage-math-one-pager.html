Coverage Math One-Pager

The whole idea in one sentence: a swap is safe when it moves hours around without changing the totals — same team hours, every shift still at minimum staffing, nobody pushed into overtime.

Check 1 — Hours balance (zero-sum test)

Total hours given by Person A must equal the hours A receives; same for B. The team's total doesn't move — just the labels change.

Quick share of weekly load

One 12-hour shift on a standard 3 × 12 week is 33.3% of your weekly hours, so a straight 12-for-12 swap moves exactly one shift's share of workload each way. Balanced swaps never move more.

Red flags (say “no” to yourself before asking)

  • The swap gives one person an overloaded two-week stretch.
  • One of the two shifts drops below minimum or the right skill mix.
  • “We'll figure out the return shift later” — that's a debt, not a swap.
  • A weekends/rotation promise with no date attached.
  • Anyone says “it's fine, nobody checks” — someone always checks.

What break-even is NOT

  • Trading hours unevenly “plus a favor.” The math is the contract.
  • Swapping a holiday for a regular day without extra agreement — holidays have their own fairness rules; treat them as separate negotiations.
  • Quietly changing differentials (night ↔ day) without both people acknowledging the pay shift.
Pocket summary: zero-sum for people. Non-negative for staffing. Nobody into overtime. If all three hold, ask with confidence. If one doesn't, rework it before asking.
