Shift-Swap Request Emails (Colleague + Manager)

The text gets the yes. The email creates the paper trail your manager, scheduler, and — worst case — HR can read later. A swap that isn't documented didn't happen.

Structure that works

  1. Subject line with dates — schedulers triage by subject.
  2. The trade, stated as math: I cover X, you cover Y.
  3. Status of approvals — who has said yes so far.
  4. A clear ask: “Reply all confirming.”

Email 1 — To Your Colleague (after the text)

Subject: Shift swap with you — [My initials] ↔ [Their initials], [Date] for [Date]

Hi [Name],

Following up on my text — thanks for being open to this! To make it
easy to log, here's the whole swap in one place:

  I give:  [Tue 3/5, 1900-0700, my shift]
  I get:   [Fri 3/8, 1900-0700, your shift]

Both shifts stay in the same pay period, and I've checked the
coverage math (same hours, no overtime created). Could you reply-all
to confirm, and I'll send the approval request to [Charge nurse or
manager] right after?

Thanks so much — you're saving me during a week I really needed.

[Your name]

Email 2 — The Manager Approval Request (the one that matters)

Subject: Shift swap request — [Name] ↔ [Name], [Date] for [Date]

Hi [Manager name],

[Colleague] and I would like to swap shifts and I want to make sure
it follows procedure before it goes on the schedule:

  Swap: [Name A] covers [Date, Shift], [Name B] covers [Date, Shift]
  Coverage: Both shifts meet standard staffing for the unit; no
  overtime or differential changes for either of us.
  Both of us have confirmed via email (thread attached / below).

Could you approve this or let me know what else you need? Happy to
change the dates if a different combo works better.

Thank you,
[Your name], [Role/initials]
Pro move: reply-all to your colleague's confirmation thread instead of starting a new email. The whole history travels with the request, so your manager approves with two clicks instead of five questions.

Email 3 — Short Follow-Up (48 hours, friendly)

Hi [Manager name], just floating this back up — I know schedules are
busy. If it's easier to say no or adjust dates, that's totally fine;
I just don't want either of us stuck without a heads-up before
[the schedule posts / my shift].

Email 4 — The Graceful Decline (when you're being asked)

Hi [Name], I really appreciate you thinking of me, but that week
doesn't work for me. Would [alternate date] work instead? If not,
no stress at all — hope you find someone quickly.

Email etiquette mini-checklist

  • One swap per thread — schedulers hate mix-and-match puzzles.
  • Use consistent hour format (1900-0700 beats “7pm-ish”).
  • Keep the thread — “below” beats memory.
  • Get the manager's written okay before telling anyone it's official.
